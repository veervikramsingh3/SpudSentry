{% elif result.disease == 'Early Blight' %}
Early Blight Information

Early blight (Alternaria solani) is a fungal disease that affects potato plants, particularly during warm, humid weather. It typically begins on older, lower leaves and progresses upward.

The characteristic "target" or "bullseye" pattern of concentric rings within lesions is a key identifier of this disease.

Management Strategies
  • Remove and destroy infected plant material
  • Apply fungicides containing chlorothalonil or copper
  • Improve air circulation through proper plant spacing
  • Water at the base of plants to keep foliage dry
  • Rotate crops - avoid planting potatoes or tomatoes in the same location for 2-3 years
Preventative Measures
  • Use certified disease-free seed potatoes
  • Plant resistant varieties when available
  • Maintain balanced soil nutrition
  • Apply preventative fungicides before disease appears
  • Avoid overhead irrigation which promotes leaf wetness
{% elif result.disease == 'Late Blight' %}
Late Blight Information

Late blight (Phytophthora infestans) is a devastating disease that caused the Irish Potato Famine. It spreads rapidly in cool, wet conditions and can destroy entire fields in days if not controlled.

The pathogen can affect all parts of the plant including stems, leaves, and tubers.

Urgent Control Measures
  • Apply appropriate fungicides immediately
  • Remove and destroy infected plants to prevent spread
  • Create a buffer zone around infected areas
  • Harvest remaining potatoes as soon as possible
  • Monitor weather forecasts for high-risk conditions
Long-term Management
  • Plant resistant varieties
  • Use preventative fungicides during high-risk weather
  • Properly dispose of cull piles and volunteer plants
  • Improve field drainage to reduce humidity
  • Practice crop rotation with 3-4 year cycles
